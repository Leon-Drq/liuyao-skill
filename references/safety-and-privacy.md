# Safety and privacy

## High-stakes questions

Traditional readings may help reflection but cannot determine medical diagnosis, legal rights, investment suitability, emergency response, pregnancy, death, crime, or another person's dangerousness. Put urgent real-world action first. Do not give exact death, disaster, illness, return, price, or winning-date predictions.

## Consent and third parties

Use only data the user is authorized to share. Relationship analysis may discuss interaction patterns from supplied information; it must not claim access to another person's thoughts, fidelity, diagnosis, sexuality, or intent.

## Images

Ask the user to remove EXIF/location data when practical and avoid storing raw photos. Analyze only requested visible features. Do not identify a person or infer race, ethnicity, religion, health condition, disability, sexuality, political belief, criminality, or other sensitive traits from appearance.

## Birth data and records

Treat precise birth time, location, names, photos, and relationship histories as sensitive personal data. Keep them out of source control, examples, telemetry, and public issue reports. Use synthetic examples.

## Secrets

Never embed API keys, access tokens, cookies, private prompts, or database credentials. Read authentication only from environment variables or the host's secret store. If a real credential appears in tracked documentation or history, remove it and rotate it before publication.
